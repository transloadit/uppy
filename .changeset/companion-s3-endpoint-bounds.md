Security: bound the work and memory the S3 upload endpoints spend on a client. `GET /s3/multipart/:uploadId/batch` signs at most 100 part numbers per request, and refuses part numbers that are not whole numbers from 1 to 10000. No current Uppy client uses this route; `@uppy/aws-s3-multipart` releases from 2021 and 2022 did, with batches no larger than their `limit` option (5 by default). Listing the parts of an upload stops, and cancels its S3 request, once the client has disconnected; each connection lists one upload at a time, starting the next listing only once the previous answer has been sent; and at most 4 listings longer than 1000 parts page through S3 at once (shorter listings never wait). If you set `s3.conditions`, check them before upgrading: multipart uploads that break them used to succeed and now fail. `s3.conditions` is now partly applied to multipart uploads: a Content-Type that the conditions refuse is rejected when the upload is created, and with a `content-length-range` condition Companion checks the total size of the parts on completion and aborts uploads outside the range. `/s3/sts` and uploads from providers and URLs still ignore `s3.conditions`.

If you set a `content-length-range` condition: completing a multipart upload now lists its parts first, so Companion's AWS credentials need the `s3:ListMultipartUploadParts` permission (already needed to resume uploads, and listed in the `@uppy/aws-s3` docs). Without it, multipart uploads fail on completion. A `content-length-range` condition whose minimum and maximum aren't sizes with minimum <= maximum is now refused at startup.
